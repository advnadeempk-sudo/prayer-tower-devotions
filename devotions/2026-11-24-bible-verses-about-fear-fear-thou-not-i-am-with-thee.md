# Bible Verses About Fear: Fear Thou Not, for I Am With Thee

**Scripture:** Fear thou not; for I am with thee: be not dismayed; for I am thy God: I will strengthen thee; yea, I will help thee; yea, I will uphold thee with the right hand of my righteousness. (Isaiah 41:10, KJV)

Of all the Bible verses about fear, Isaiah 41:10 is the one most believers reach for first, and for good reason. It was spoken to a small people surrounded by great empires, with exile on the horizon and every natural reason to be afraid. God does not deny the danger. He answers it with Himself.

Look at the shape of the verse. Two commands: fear thou not, be not dismayed. Two reasons: for I am with thee, for I am thy God. Then three promises, each beginning with "I will": I will strengthen thee, I will help thee, I will uphold thee. Fear whispers, "You are alone." God says, "I am with thee." Fear whispers, "You cannot stand." God says, "I will uphold thee."

The Hebrew word translated "uphold" is tamak. It means to grasp, to hold fast, to keep a firm grip on something so that it does not fall. Picture a father crossing a busy Lahore road with a small child. The child's grip is weak, but the father's is not. God does not simply tell us to be brave; He takes hold of us with "the right hand of my righteousness", the hand of His power and His faithfulness to His own promise.

Here is the truth for today: courage in the Bible is never the absence of fear. It is the presence of God. The command "fear not" would be cruel if it stood alone. It stands on the word "for": for I am with thee. We are not asked to feel strong. We are asked to look at who is holding us.

I remember a sister who came to the prayer room after a frightening medical report. "Pastor," she said, "I keep telling myself not to be afraid, and it does not work." We read Isaiah 41:10 together, and I asked her to stop at every "for". Fear does not leave because we scold it; it leaves when we fix our eyes on the One who has hold of us. That night she wrote two lines inside her Bible: "God is able. God is faithful." The report did not change that week, but her nights did.

So when fear knocks today, do three things. Name the fear honestly before God; He already knows it. Read this verse aloud and put your own name where it says "thee". Then take the one step of obedience you have been afraid to take, because faith grows by acting on the promise, not by waiting until the feeling of fear has gone.

In a 24-hour prayer tower someone is awake in every watch of the night, and night is when fear speaks loudest. Many of our brothers and sisters in hostile places read "fear thou not" in a very literal way: a knock at the door, a job suddenly lost, a child under pressure at school. When you take your hour, you place one hand in God's and reach the other toward them. The hand that upholds you upholds them too.

## Prayer

Father, You have said, "Fear thou not, for I am with thee." I bring You my fears by name today, the ones I speak of and the ones I hide. I choose to believe that You are able and You are faithful. Strengthen me when I am weak, help me when I cannot see the way, and uphold me with the right hand of Your righteousness. When fear knocks again, remind me that You are holding my hand. Give the same courage to my brothers and sisters who suffer for Your name. In the name of Jesus Christ I pray. Amen.
