# Worship in Spirit and in Truth: The Worshippers the Father Is Seeking

**Date:** Sunday, 8 November 2026 · **Theme:** Worship, revival & the Word

**Scripture:** *"But the hour cometh, and now is, when the true worshippers shall worship the Father in spirit and in truth: for the Father seeketh such to worship him. God is a Spirit: and they that worship him must worship him in spirit and in truth."* — John 4:23-24 (KJV)

What does it mean to worship in spirit and in truth? Jesus gave the answer not in a temple but beside a village well, to a woman with a broken past who wanted to argue about which mountain was the right place to worship. His reply turned her question around. The Father is not searching for the right place; He is searching for the right people. "The Father seeketh such to worship him." On this Lord's Day, that is the question that matters: is He finding one in me?

The Greek word for worship here is proskuneo. It joins two small words: pros, "toward", and kuneo, "to kiss". It pictures a person bowing low and kissing toward a king, the posture of a heart that honours someone far above itself. Worship, then, is not first a song or a service. It is giving God His worth: bowing the whole heart toward Him in love and reverence, so that He is treasured above everything else.

"In spirit" means worship rises from the inside, from the place where the Holy Spirit has made us alive, not from a performance on the outside. "In truth" means it is honest, and anchored in who God really is as He has shown Himself in His Word and in Jesus Christ. Spirit without truth drifts into feelings; truth without spirit grows cold and formal. The Father seeks both together.

Scripture shows that this kind of worship is also a weapon and a doorway. Paul and Silas, beaten and chained at midnight, prayed and sang praises to God, and the prison shook and the doors opened. When Judah faced an army it could not defeat, the king sent the singers out ahead of the soldiers, and as they began to praise, the Lord Himself fought the battle. Notice the order. Praise did not follow the victory; it came before it. People who worship in spirit and in truth do not wait for their circumstances to be right before they bow.

So how do you live this today? Begin at home before you go to church. Thank God aloud for three things. Read one Psalm. Sing one song, even if your voice is weak and no one else is awake. Make worship the daily climate of your house, and watch the atmosphere change: fear, quarrelling and heaviness find no comfortable room where praise lives. Then gather with God's people, for the Word tells us not to forsake the assembling of ourselves together.

This is the heartbeat of the 24-hour prayer tower. In every hour of the day and night someone is bowing toward the Father, and worship comes before the requests. Across the world, believers who cannot gather openly are worshipping right now: a whispered Psalm, a song sung softly behind a closed door, a heart kissing toward the King when no building is safe. Their worship in spirit and in truth needs no temple, and heaven hears every word. When you take your hour, you join them.

## Prayer

Father, I bow my heart toward You today. You are Spirit and You are true, and I cannot worship You with my lips alone. Fill me with Your Holy Spirit so that my worship rises from within, and anchor me in Your Word so that it is true. Forgive me for the days I praised You only when things were going well. Teach me to sing before the battle, like Judah's singers, and at midnight, like Paul and Silas. Make my home a house of praise and my whole life an act of worship to You. In Jesus' name, Amen.
