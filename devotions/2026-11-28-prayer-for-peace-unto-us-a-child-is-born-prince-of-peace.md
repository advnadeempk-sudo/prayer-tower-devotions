# Prayer for Peace: Unto Us a Child Is Born, the Prince of Peace

**Scripture:** For unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given: and the government shall be upon his shoulder: and his name shall be called Wonderful, Counsellor, The mighty God, The everlasting Father, The Prince of Peace. Of the increase of his government and peace there shall be no end, upon the throne of David, and upon his kingdom, to order it, and to establish it with judgment and with justice from henceforth even for ever. The zeal of the LORD of hosts will perform this. (Isaiah 9:6-7, KJV)

Tomorrow the Church around the world lights the first candle of Advent, and in a world full of noise, war and worry, a prayer for peace is one of the most searched-for prayers on earth. People in every nation are asking the same question: where can peace be found? Seven hundred years before Bethlehem, God answered it through the prophet Isaiah. A child would be born. A son would be given. And one of His names would be The Prince of Peace.

The Hebrew word here is shalom. In English we hear "peace" and think of silence, or the absence of war. Shalom is far bigger. It means wholeness, soundness, everything in its right place: a body healed, a family reconciled, a debt settled, a nation at rest. When Isaiah calls the coming King Sar Shalom, the Prince of Peace, he means the One who has the authority to make broken things whole. And verse 7 tells us His peace never runs out: "of the increase of his government and peace there shall be no end."

Notice where that peace rests: "the government shall be upon his shoulder." Not on the shoulders of armies or parliaments, and not on yours. The weight of the world is carried by Christ. That is why a Christian can pray for peace with confidence rather than despair. We are not begging a weak God to hold back a strong darkness. We are asking the reigning Prince to extend a government that is already growing.

I think of a mother in a small village who, after years of fear on her street, began inviting her neighbours to sit on her veranda every evening to pray for the peace of their lane. Nothing changed in a week. But over the months, quarrels cooled, a feud between two families ended, and one hardened man said, "There is something in this house that is not in mine." That something was shalom, and it had a name.

Here is how to live it today. First, receive His peace before you pray for the world's peace; Jesus said, "my peace I give unto you" (John 14:27). Second, pray for peace specifically: name your home, your street, your city and your nation. Scripture tells us to pray "for kings, and for all that are in authority; that we may lead a quiet and peaceable life in all godliness and honesty" (1 Timothy 2:2). Third, be a peacemaker where you stand. The peace you carry to the kitchen table and the workplace is the Prince's government increasing.

This is the heartbeat of the 24-hour Prayer Tower. Every hour, somewhere in the world, a believer stands before the throne asking for shalom over nations that have only known conflict, and over brothers and sisters who worship under threat. In our own land, Revival Church International is preparing a Christmas Prayer Festival whose message is simple: love, peace and harmony from Pakistan to the whole world. Peace is not a slogan; it is a Person, and He is still giving Himself to the nations.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, Prince of Peace, I receive Your shalom today. Quiet my anxious heart and make whole what is broken in me. I pray for peace in my home, my street, my city and my nation; let Your government increase where there has been fear and strife. Give wisdom to all in authority, that we may live quiet and peaceable lives. Protect my brothers and sisters who worship under threat, and let the nations see that You alone can make all things whole. Use me as a peacemaker where I stand. In Your mighty name, Amen.

## Pray with us today

- For the Prince of Peace to bring shalom to nations torn by war and unrest, and to every home where fear has moved in.
- For persecuted believers who gather in secret or under threat, that the peace which passes understanding would guard their hearts and God would guard their lives.
- For the Christmas Prayer Festival and every Advent gathering, that thousands would meet Jesus and carry His peace home.
