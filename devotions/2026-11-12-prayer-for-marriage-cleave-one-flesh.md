# Prayer for Marriage: Cleave to One Another and Let No Man Put Asunder

**Daily Devotional — Thursday, 12 November 2026**

**Scripture:** *"Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh."* — Genesis 2:24 (KJV)

**Read:** Genesis 2:18–25 · Mark 10:6–9 · Ephesians 5:22–33 · 1 Peter 3:1–7 · Ecclesiastes 4:9–12

---

Every marriage reaches a season when the wisest thing left to do is pray. People type "prayer for marriage" into their phones when the conversation at the table has gone quiet, when money is short, when the children have begun to notice. God's first word about marriage was spoken before any of those troubles existed: "Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh" (Genesis 2:24). Jesus quoted it and added, "What therefore God hath joined together, let not man put asunder" (Mark 10:9).

The Hebrew word for "cleave" is dabaq. It means to stick fast, to cling, to be glued so tightly that two things can no longer be pulled apart. It is the word used when Ruth "clave unto" Naomi while her sister turned back, and when Israel is told to "cleave unto the LORD" (Deuteronomy 10:20). Cleaving is not a feeling that comes and goes. It is a decision to hold on. A marriage is not kept by romance alone; it is kept by two people who keep choosing to cling, with God's own hands pressed around them.

Think of two pieces of wood joined by a carpenter. While the glue is wet, the joint can be pulled apart with one hand, so the carpenter clamps them overnight. In the morning the joint is stronger than the wood on either side. Many marriages come apart because the couple let go during the clamping season, when love is tested by tiredness, bills, in-laws and silence. Prayer is the clamp. When husband and wife kneel together, God holds the joint still until it sets.

I have said to fathers for many years that the home is the first church and the building is only the second. If the atmosphere of a marriage is distrust, the whole family breathes distrust; if the marriage is full of anger, the children learn anger. But where husband and wife pray together, the children breathe peace. Peter adds a sobering line: a husband who fails to honour his wife will find his prayers hindered (1 Peter 3:7). So tonight, take your spouse's hand and pray one sentence aloud for each other. Apologise for the quarrel the children overheard. Thank God for the one you chose. Five minutes a day builds a wall inside the home that no storm outside can breach.

This is why marriages are prayed for every hour at the 24-hour prayer tower. Somewhere right now a wife is praying alone for a husband who has stopped praying; a husband is praying for a wife worn down by sickness; and in hostile lands believing couples are holding on to each other and to Christ when faith has cost them their work, their home or their safety. "A threefold cord is not quickly broken" (Ecclesiastes 4:12): husband, wife, and the Lord between them. When you take your hour, you become part of that cord for a couple you will never meet.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, You joined us together, and what You have joined, let nothing put asunder. Teach us to cleave: to hold on when feelings fade, to forgive quickly and to speak gently. Where distrust or anger has entered our home, drive it out by Your Spirit. Make our marriage the first church our children see, and let them hear us pray. Heal every marriage under strain today, and hold together the couples who are suffering for Your name. Be the third cord that cannot be broken. In Your name, Amen.

## Pray with us today

- Your marriage and the marriages in your family, that husband and wife would cleave to each other and to Christ, and that old quarrels would be forgiven.
- Persecuted believers, that couples who suffer for the name of Jesus would be kept as one flesh under pressure, and that no threat or loss would put asunder what God has joined.
- The nations and Pakistan, that Christian homes would become the first church, raising children who have seen their parents pray.
