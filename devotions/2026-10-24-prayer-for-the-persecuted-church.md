# Daily Devotional — Saturday, 24 October 2026

## Prayer for the Persecuted Church: Remember Them That Are in Bonds

**Scripture:** *"Remember them that are in bonds, as bound with them; and them which suffer adversity, as being yourselves also in the body."* — Hebrews 13:3 (KJV)

**Read:** Hebrews 13:1–3 · Acts 12:1–17 · Acts 16:25–34 · Matthew 5:10–12

---

Prayer for the persecuted church is not an optional extra for a few mission-minded believers; it is a command written into the New Testament. The writer to the Hebrews tells us to remember those in bonds *"as bound with them"*, and those who suffer adversity *"as being yourselves also in the body"*. Somewhere today a brother is sitting in a cell for the name of Jesus. Somewhere a sister has lost her home, her work or her family because she would not deny her Lord. Scripture does not ask us to pity them from a distance. It asks us to stand in the chains beside them.

The Greek word translated "bound with them" is *sundedemenoi*, from *sundeō* — to be tied together, to be chained to another as a fellow prisoner. This is the only place in the New Testament where the word appears. The picture is two prisoners fastened to one chain: when one moves, the other feels it. That is how God wants His church to feel about its suffering members. *"Whether one member suffer, all the members suffer with it"* (1 Corinthians 12:26). Persecution is an injustice that offends the God of love, justice and mercy, yet Jesus called the persecuted blessed and promised them a great reward in heaven (Matthew 5:10–12).

In Acts 12 the apostle Peter was locked between two soldiers, chained, with guards at the door. The church had no lawyer, no influence and no key. But Luke records: *"prayer was made without ceasing of the church unto God for him"* (Acts 12:5). While they prayed through the night, an angel walked into the prison, the chains fell off and the iron gate opened of its own accord. The believers were so astonished when Peter knocked at the door that they thought it was his angel. Our prayers reach places our feet can never go.

So how do we pray for the persecuted church today? Pray that they will speak the gospel with boldness, as Paul asked for himself from his own prison (Ephesians 6:19–20). Pray that God's grace will be sufficient in their weakness (2 Corinthians 12:9). Pray for the wives, husbands and children left behind. And pray for the persecutors, because Jesus commanded us to bless and not to curse (Romans 12:14). The jailer at Philippi who beat Paul and Silas was washing their wounds and believing in Christ before the night was over. Many persecuted believers do not ask us to pray that trouble will end; they ask us to pray that they will be faithful in it.

This is why the 24-hour prayer tower matters. The church in Acts 12 prayed through the night, and the tower is that praying house stretched across every time zone. When a believer in a hostile place cannot gather, cannot sing and cannot pray aloud, someone in the tower is lifting them to the Father at that very hour. That is *sundedemenoi* in practice: bound together with them across the miles. In Pakistan we know what it is to be a small flock among many, and we also know the comfort of learning that strangers around the world were praying for us.

---

### Prayer for Today

Father, today I remember my brothers and sisters in bonds as if I were chained beside them. Give the imprisoned Your peace, give the grieving Your comfort, and give every believer who stands before judges the boldness of Peter and Paul. Open iron gates that no human hand can open. Turn the hearts of persecutors as You turned the jailer at Philippi. Forgive me for the days I forgot them, and make my prayers a chain of love that reaches every hidden church on earth. Strengthen every intercessor keeping watch in the prayer tower this hour. In Jesus' name, Amen.

### Pray With Us Today

- Believers imprisoned or on trial for their faith — strength, boldness and release
- The families of the persecuted, and every request submitted to the tower in the last 24 hours
- The nations where following Jesus is costly, Pakistan among them — that persecutors meet Christ as the jailer at Philippi did
