# Trust in the Lord with All Your Heart: Resting Your Whole Weight on God

**Daily Devotional — Tuesday, 27 October 2026**

**Scripture:** *"Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding. In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths."* — Proverbs 3:5-6 (KJV)

**Read:** Proverbs 3:1–12 · Psalm 56:1–4 · Isaiah 26:3–4 · Romans 4:18–21 · Proverbs 29:25

---

"Trust in the Lord with all your heart" is one of the most quoted verses in the Bible, yet one of the hardest to obey on an ordinary Tuesday when the bill is unpaid, the medical report is bad or the decision cannot wait. Proverbs 3:5-6 does not ask for a feeling. It asks for a transfer of weight: "Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding."

The Hebrew word for trust here is batach. It pictures a person hurrying to a safe place and lying down there, resting their whole weight, unafraid. David uses it in Psalm 56:3: "What time I am afraid, I will trust in thee." So batach is not the absence of fear; it is what you do with your fear. You carry it to the Lord, and you lie down on Him.

Notice the contrast. We are to trust the Lord with all the heart, the whole inner person: mind, will and emotions. We are not to lean on our own understanding. To lean is to put your weight on a staff. Our understanding is God's gift, but it was never built to carry a life. Isaiah compares trusting in human power to leaning on a bruised reed that pierces the hand (Isaiah 36:6). Many of us are limping because we leaned on something never meant to hold us.

In our villages an old rope bed, the charpai, tells the truth about trust. A guest who doubts it sits on the edge, ready to jump. A guest who trusts it lies down and sleeps. Same bed; only one is resting. Faith, as I often tell new believers, is settled trust in a God who cannot lie: resting your entire weight on Jesus, His death for your forgiveness, His resurrection for your life, His lordship over your future. Abraham faced the facts of his old body honestly and was still "fully persuaded that, what he had promised, he was able also to perform" (Romans 4:21). Two convictions held him: God is able, and God is faithful.

How do you trust in the Lord with all your heart today? Name what you have been leaning on: a salary, a relative with influence, your own cleverness. Acknowledge Him "in all thy ways", bringing the decision to Him before you make it, not after. Take the promise as it stands: "he shall direct thy paths." Then write beside your mountain the two convictions that held Abraham: God is able. God is faithful.
