# How to Pray for Others: The LORD Turned the Captivity of Job When He Prayed for His Friends

**Scripture:** And the LORD turned the captivity of Job, when he prayed for his friends: also the LORD gave Job twice as much as he had before. (Job 42:10, KJV)

Many believers want to know how to pray for others, and the last chapter of Job gives a surprising lesson. After forty chapters of questions, tears and arguments, God never explains to Job why he suffered. Instead He gives Job a task: pray for the three friends who sat beside you and accused you. And the moment Job obeys, his own story turns.

Notice the order of events. Job had already repented in dust and ashes, yet the restoration did not begin there. It began "when he prayed for his friends." The man who had been pouring out his own complaints now stands before God as a priest for the very people who had rubbed salt into his wounds.

The Hebrew phrase translated "turned the captivity" is shub shebuth. Job was never in a prison or carried off to a foreign land. The words picture God bringing a person back from the far country of loss and setting his life right again. Job's captivity was grief, sickness, shame and the bitterness that grows when friends fail you, and God turned every part of it around.

Here is the truth for today: intercession is a door, and God often opens it from the other side. While we hold on to our own hurts, our own captivity continues. When we hand other people over to God in prayer, especially people who cannot or will not pray for themselves, God is free to change our condition too.

I think of a brother who was cheated by a relative over a small piece of family land. For years every family gathering reopened the wound. One morning in the prayer room he did something new. He spoke that relative's name aloud and asked God to bless him. Nothing changed outside at first, but the chains inside fell off, and in time the relationship itself began to heal. Mercy came to Job when he prayed for his friends, not while he argued with them.

So how do you pray for others? Pray for people by name, not in vague generalities. Pray for those who cannot pray for themselves: the sick, the backslidden, the unbeliever in your own house. Ask God to give them what you yourself most need from Him: mercy, light and peace. And when someone's words have wounded you, do not wait until you feel like it. Pray for them first and let the feelings follow.

This is why a 24-hour prayer tower exists. Every hour of the day and night someone is standing in the gap for people they may never meet: a sick child in another city, a family under pressure, believers in a hostile village who cannot gather openly. When the persecuted church prays for those who oppose it, it is walking the path of Job, and heaven answers with a restoration no human court can order.

Whose name do you need to speak before God today? Say it now, ask Him to bless that person, and then watch what the LORD does with your captivity.

## Prayer

Heavenly Father, thank You that You are the God who turns captivity around. I confess that I have held on to complaints against You and bitterness against people. Today I lay them at Your feet. I name before You those who have hurt me, and I ask You to bless them with Your mercy and light. Teach me to stand in the gap for others as Job did, for the sick, the lost and the weary. Turn my captivity, Lord, restore what was lost, and let my life show Your tender mercy. In the name of Jesus Christ I pray. Amen.
