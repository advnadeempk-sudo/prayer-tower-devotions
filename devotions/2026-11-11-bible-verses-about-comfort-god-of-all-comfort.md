# Bible Verses About Comfort: The God of All Comfort Who Comforts Us in Our Tribulation

**Daily Devotional — Wednesday, 11 November 2026**

**Scripture:** *"Blessed be God, even the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort; Who comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God."* — 2 Corinthians 1:3-4 (KJV)

**Read:** 2 Corinthians 1:3–7 · 2 Corinthians 7:5–7 · Psalm 34:18 · John 14:16–18 · Isaiah 40:1

---

People search for Bible verses about comfort late at night: a phone call has come, a diagnosis has been read, a chair at the table is empty. The Bible answers that moment not with a slogan but with a name: "Blessed be God, even the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort; who comforteth us in all our tribulation" (2 Corinthians 1:3-4). Before God gives you comfort, He tells you who He is. Not the God of some comfort. The God of all of it.

The Greek word behind "comfort" is paraklesis. It comes from a verb meaning "to call alongside," the same family of words Jesus used when He promised the Holy Spirit as the Comforter, the One called to stand beside us. Biblical comfort is not a soft blanket thrown over a problem. It is a Person who stands next to you. The word was also used for strengthening a soldier before battle. God's comfort does not only dry your tears; it puts strength back into your legs.

Paul knew this from the inside. He admits that in Asia he was "pressed out of measure, above strength, insomuch that we despaired even of life." Yet in chapter 7 he tells how the comfort came: "God, that comforteth those that are cast down, comforted us by the coming of Titus." No angel, no vision. A brother walked through the door. God often comforts us with a face, a voice, a hand on the shoulder. "The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart" (Psalm 34:18). Nigh means near. Close enough to touch.

Think of a mother sitting through the night beside a child burning with fever. She cannot remove the fever with her hands, but she will not leave the bedside. In the morning the child remembers one thing: she was there. That is paraklesis. God never promised us a life without storms; He promised to be with us in the storm. The anchor does not stop the waves. It holds the boat steady until morning.

Now notice why God comforts you: "that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble." Comfort is given to be given away. The tears you cried last year are your qualification for the visit you will make this week. If you have been comforted in grief, you know what to say to the grieving, and what not to say. Do not waste your sorrow. Turn it into a ministry.

This is the heartbeat of the 24-hour prayer tower. Every hour, somewhere in the world, a believer sits beside a stranger in prayer and becomes Titus to them. Every hour we carry our persecuted brothers and sisters, pressed out of measure for the name of Jesus, before the Father of mercies. A brother cannot always walk through their door, but the Church can stand alongside them in prayer, and the God of all comfort stands with them in their cell and their fear.

## Prayer

Father of mercies and God of all comfort, I bring You my broken places today. Come alongside me as You have promised. Dry my tears and put strength back into my legs. Thank You that You are near to the brokenhearted and that You have never left me in the storm. Now make me a comforter. Show me one person this week who needs the comfort You have given me, and give me the courage to go. Stand beside my brothers and sisters who suffer for Your name, and let the nations find their comfort in Jesus. In His name, Amen.
