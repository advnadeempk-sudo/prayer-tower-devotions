# Daily Devotional — Saturday, 21 November 2026

## Pray for Those Who Persecute You: Love Your Enemies and Bless Them That Curse You

**Scripture:** *"But I say unto you, Love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you, and pray for them which despitefully use you, and persecute you;"* — Matthew 5:44 (KJV)

**Read:** Matthew 5:43–48 · Luke 6:27–36 · Luke 23:34 · Acts 7:54–60 · Romans 12:14–21 · Philippians 3:12–14

---

Pray for those who persecute you. It is one of the hardest things Jesus ever asked, and He asked it of people who knew exactly what persecution felt like. In the Sermon on the Mount He climbs step by step: love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you, and then the summit, "pray for them which despitefully use you, and persecute you." Love begins in the heart, blessing uses the tongue, doing good uses the hands, but prayer brings your enemy before the throne of God.

The Greek word for persecute is diōkō. It means to chase, to hunt someone down the way a hunter pursues his prey. Yet the New Testament uses the very same word in a beautiful way. Paul writes, "I press toward the mark for the prize of the high calling of God in Christ Jesus" (Philippians 3:14), and the word for press is diōkō. The man who once hunted believers from city to city spent the rest of his life hunting after Christ with the same energy. God did not destroy the chaser; He turned him around.

As Stephen was being stoned he knelt and cried, "Lord, lay not this sin to their charge" (Acts 7:60). A young man named Saul stood nearby, consenting to his death. The persecutor who heard that prayer became the apostle Paul. Stephen was only echoing his Master, who prayed from the cross, "Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do" (Luke 23:34).

Why is prayer the highest form of love for an enemy? Because every other door may be shut. You may not be allowed to speak to him or do him good. But you can always pray, and prayer takes your enemy out of your hands and places him in God's. Praying for someone does not mean approving of what he does. It means refusing to let his hatred decide what kind of person you will become.

Picture a believer whose small shop was shut down after a neighbour spread lies about him. Every night, when the anger rose, he spoke that neighbour's name to God and asked for his blessing and salvation. Years later the neighbour, in trouble of his own, found the believer's door the only one in the street still open to him. Bitterness would have closed that door. Prayer kept it open.

Today, write down one name. Then pray three things: that God would bless that person and not merely stop them; that they would meet Jesus as Saul met Him; and that your own heart would be guarded from bitterness, which is persecution's second wound.

Somewhere tonight a brother or sister is praying this very prayer for people who threatened them this week. In the 24-Hour Digital Prayer Tower, intercessors across every time zone stand with them, so that the fire on the altar never goes out (Leviticus 6:13). When you take your hour, pray for the persecuted Church, and dare to pray for their persecutors too, because some of the gospel's greatest victories came when an enemy became a brother.

**Prayer**

Lord Jesus, You prayed for the men who nailed You to the cross, and You ask me to pray for those who have hurt me. I cannot do this in my own strength, so fill my heart with Your love. I bring before You the people who have cursed me, lied about me or treated me with spite. Bless them, Lord. Open their eyes to see You as Saul saw You on the road, and turn their chasing into following. Guard my heart from bitterness and make me a peacemaker. Strengthen my persecuted brothers and sisters tonight, and let their love for their enemies become a testimony that wins many to You. In Your name, Amen.
