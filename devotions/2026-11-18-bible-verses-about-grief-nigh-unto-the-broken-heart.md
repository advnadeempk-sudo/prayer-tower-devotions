# Bible Verses About Grief: The LORD Is Nigh Unto Them That Are of a Broken Heart

**Date:** 2026-11-18 · **Focus keyword:** Bible verses about grief

## Scripture

> The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit. Many are the afflictions of the righteous: but the LORD delivereth him out of them all. (Psalm 34:18-19, KJV)

## Devotion

When sorrow comes, many of us open the Bible looking for Bible verses about grief, and one of the first we find is Psalm 34:18. David wrote this psalm as a fugitive, hunted and homeless, having just escaped a king's court by pretending to be out of his mind. Out of that low place he handed every grieving soul a promise to hold: "The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart."

The Hebrew word for "nigh" is qarob. It is the word used for a close relative, a neighbour next door, something within arm's reach. It does not describe a God who watches from a distance; it describes a God close enough to touch. Notice who He is near to. Not the strong or the composed, not those who have finished crying, but the broken, pictured as a clay pot shattered on the floor, and the crushed in spirit, ground down like powder. When your heart lies in pieces, God does not stand back until you recover. The pieces are exactly where He draws close.

Think of the day Moses died. Israel wept for thirty days. On Mount Nebo no human being could go with him: Aaron had gone before, Joshua and the people stayed below. Yet Scripture says God Himself buried him. Where no friend could stand beside Moses, God stood. That is the pattern of grace. The valley of the shadow of death is the one road none of us can walk with anyone else, and it is precisely there that the Shepherd says, "I am with thee."

Think of Job on the ash heap: his children gone, his body sick, his friends accusing. God never told him why. Instead God spoke to him out of the whirlwind, so that the storm itself became the meeting place. Job did not get an answer to "why"; he received a sight of "Who". Many believers testify to the same: the darkest days of our lives were the days we knew His nearness most clearly.

So today, if you are grieving, do not hide the broken pieces from God or pretend in prayer that you are fine. Tell Him plainly what you have lost and how much it hurts. Read Psalm 34 aloud, slowly. Let the church be your Aaron and Hur, for God often brings His nearness through the hands of brothers and sisters who hold yours. And if you are not grieving today, find someone who is and simply sit with them. Be the nearness of God to them.

This is why the Prayer Tower never sleeps. At this very hour, somewhere in the world, a mother is burying her child, a widow is facing her first night alone, and a believer in a hostile land is mourning a loved one lost for the sake of Christ, unable even to gather openly and weep. Around the clock someone at the tower stands before God for them, so that no broken heart in any nation goes unprayed for. The LORD is nigh; we keep watch with Him.

## Prayer

Father, You are near to the broken-hearted, and today I bring You my broken heart. I will not pretend before You. You know what I have lost and how heavy it is. Draw close, as You promised, closer than my sorrow. Where no one else can walk with me, walk with me. Speak to me out of this storm, and let me see You even when I cannot understand. Heal what is shattered, lift what is crushed, and give me grace to hold the hand of someone else who is grieving. In the name of Jesus, the Man of Sorrows who carried mine, Amen.
