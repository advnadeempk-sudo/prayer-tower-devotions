# Bible Verses About Persecution: The Word of God Is Not Bound

**Daily Devotional — Sunday, 1 November 2026 · International Day of Prayer for the Persecuted Church**

**Scripture:** *"Wherein I suffer trouble, as an evil doer, even unto bonds; but the word of God is not bound."* — 2 Timothy 2:9 (KJV)

**Read:** 2 Timothy 2:8–13 · Matthew 5:10–12 · John 15:18–21 · Luke 21:12–15 · Philippians 1:12–14 · 2 Thessalonians 3:1

---

Today, the first Sunday of November, churches across the world keep the International Day of Prayer for the Persecuted Church. Many believers will look for Bible verses about persecution today, and the Bible has them in plenty: "Blessed are they which are persecuted for righteousness' sake" (Matthew 5:10); "If they have persecuted me, they will also persecute you" (John 15:20); "all that will live godly in Christ Jesus shall suffer persecution" (2 Timothy 3:12). Yet one short verse, written from a Roman prison, holds them all together: "the word of God is not bound."

Paul wrote 2 Timothy in chains, treated "as an evil doer", expecting to die. The Greek word for bound is deo, to tie up or chain, the word used for a prisoner fastened to his guard. Paul puts it in the perfect tense, dedetai, which describes a settled condition: it has been bound and it stays bound. Then he denies it of the Word: ou dedetai. His wrists were in a settled state of chains. The gospel was in a settled state of freedom, and no one could change either by force.

History proved him right. From that cell came letters that have preached to every generation since. The soldiers who chained him heard the gospel, and the whole palace guard learned why he was there (Philippians 1:13). Persecution meant to silence the Word became the Word's loudspeaker. Jesus had promised exactly this: when you are dragged before rulers for His name, "it shall turn to you for a testimony" (Luke 21:13).

I think of a brother in our region who lost his job when his employer learned that he followed Jesus. For months he was crushed. Then he began meeting other jobless men at a roadside tea stall each morning, and he opened his Bible with them. Several of those men now worship with us. His livelihood was bound; the Word was not.

As a lawyer I thank God for every charter and court that protects the freedom to believe, for the law of nations rightly condemns persecution. But as a pastor I know something stronger: the Word needs no permit to travel. It passes through prison walls, closed borders and confiscated phones. So read the Bible verses about persecution not as a sad list but as promises. Do not be surprised by the fiery trial (1 Peter 4:12). Bless those who persecute you (Romans 12:14), because a blessing is a seed and a curse is only a wall. And refuse the quiet self-censorship that chains the Word before anyone else does.

This is why the 24-hour prayer tower never sleeps. Every hour someone is praying for a believer who, at that very moment, is in a cell, a court, a hostile home or a lonely workplace. Paul asked the churches to pray "that the word of the Lord may have free course" (2 Thessalonians 3:1). When you take your hour today, you are loosing chains that no key can open and sending the unbound Word where no preacher can go.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, today I remember my brothers and sisters who suffer for Your name. Be near to every believer in prison, in court, in a hostile home or a lonely workplace. Give them courage, comfort and the joy of Your presence. Let Your Word run free from every cell and every closed place, and turn their suffering into testimony. Forgive me for the times fear has bound my own witness, and make me bold and loving. I bless those who oppose Your people, and I ask You to open their hearts. Give the nations leaders who honour freedom of conscience, and let Your Word have free course in every land. In Jesus' name, Amen.

## Pray with us today

- Persecuted believers around the world, especially those in prison for Christ, that they would feel the nearness of Jesus and that the Word would run free from their cells.
- Yourself, that fear of people would never bind your witness.
- The nations and their leaders, that freedom of conscience would be honoured and the gospel would have free course.
