# Morning Prayer: Set Your Day in Order Before God and Look Up

**Daily Devotional — Monday, 2 November 2026 · Prayer & Intercession**

**Scripture:** *"My voice shalt thou hear in the morning, O LORD; in the morning will I direct my prayer unto thee, and will look up."* — Psalm 5:3 (KJV)

**Read:** Psalm 5:1–8 · Leviticus 6:8–13 · Mark 1:35 · Psalm 63:1 · Lamentations 3:22–23

---

Morning prayer is one of the oldest habits of God's people and one of the most searched-for phrases on the internet today. People sense that the first minutes of the day matter. David knew it long ago: "My voice shalt thou hear in the morning, O LORD; in the morning will I direct my prayer unto thee, and will look up." Before the palace woke, before the news of the day reached him, the king had already spoken to his God.

The word translated "direct" is the Hebrew arak. It is not a casual word. It is the word used for laying the wood in order on the altar, for setting the bread in order on the table of the Lord, and for drawing up an army in battle lines. When David says "I will direct my prayer," he means: I will lay my day out before God piece by piece, the way the priest laid the morning sacrifice in order, and then I will stand and watch for His answer.

Here is the one truth of this verse: morning prayer is not reciting words; it is arranging your life before God before anyone else arranges it for you. Whoever gets your first hour shapes your whole day. In Leviticus the priest's first task each morning was to put fresh wood on the altar, so that the fire "shall never go out" (Leviticus 6:13). The fire was God's; the arranging was the priest's. Morning prayer is laying the wood.

I think of a mother in our fellowship who rises before her household. She lays out the names of her children one by one, then her husband, then the day's work, then the bills. She once told me, "If I do not lay them before God in the morning, I carry them on my own back all day." Her home has its storms like every home, but there is a steadiness in that house that everyone notices, and it begins in the dark before dawn.

How do you live this today? First, lay it out: before you open your phone, take five minutes and name the people, the meetings, the fears and the hopes of this day, one by one, as if placing each on the altar. Second, look up: David did not pray and run; he prayed and watched, expecting God to move. Third, leave it there. What has been set in order before God need not be carried on your own shoulders. If you miss a morning, do not condemn yourself; simply lay the wood again tomorrow. The fire is His.

At the 24-hour prayer tower it is always morning somewhere. When the sun rises over Asia, intercessors there lay the first wood of the day. As the light moves across Africa, Europe and the Americas, others take up the watch, so that the fire on the altar never goes out. Somewhere this morning a believer is waking in a place where praying openly costs something, and their quiet morning prayer is the bravest act of their day. When you take your hour, you join your voice to theirs, and the Lord hears them together.

## Prayer

Lord, my voice You shall hear this morning. Before the day speaks to me, I speak to You. I lay before You my family, my work, my fears and my hopes, one by one, and set them in order on Your altar. Keep the fire burning in my heart today, and help me not to rush away but to look up and wait for You. Strengthen my brothers and sisters who pray this morning in secret and in danger, and hear their voices with mine. In Jesus' name, Amen.
