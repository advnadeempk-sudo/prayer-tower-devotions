# Bible Verses About Strength: They That Wait Upon the LORD Shall Renew Their Strength

**Scripture:** But they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings as eagles; they shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint. (Isaiah 40:31, KJV)

When people look for Bible verses about strength, Isaiah 40:31 is nearly always the first one they find, and rightly so. It was first spoken to a people worn thin by long trouble, who felt that God had forgotten them. Into that weariness the prophet speaks of the everlasting God who "fainteth not, neither is weary," and who "giveth power to the faint; and to them that have no might he increaseth strength" (Isaiah 40:28-29).

The whole promise turns on one Hebrew word: qavah, translated "wait." It does not mean sitting idle while the clock ticks. Its root picture is of twisting strands together to make a cord; the same root gives the Hebrew word for a measuring line. To wait upon the LORD is to bind your life to Him strand by strand, like a rope wound around an anchor, until your weakness is twisted together with His strength. Biblical hope is not wishful thinking. It is holding on.

Notice the order of the promise. We might expect the eagle first, then the running, then the walking, as though God's strength were shrinking. But the prophet is describing real life. There are mountain-top seasons when we soar. There are busy seasons when we run. And there are long, grey seasons when all we can do is put one foot in front of the other. The promise covers all three. God's strength is for the ordinary Tuesday, not only for the day of the miracle.

I think of a widow who, for more than twenty years, rose before dawn to pray and then worked all day to feed her children. Nobody ever saw her soar like an eagle. But she walked and did not faint, and today her children love and serve the Lord. That is renewed strength: not the absence of weariness, but a supply that keeps arriving.

How do we wait upon the LORD today? Give Him the first ten minutes of the morning before the phone takes them. Read the promise aloud and put your own name in it. When you are tired, do not quit; slow down to a walk and keep walking with Him. Tell Him plainly where you have no might left, because that is exactly the person He has promised to strengthen.

This is why the Prayer Tower never closes. Somewhere in the world it is three o'clock in the morning, and a believer who cannot gather openly is awake, praying in a whisper, tired beyond words, yet still walking and not fainting. When you take your hour, your cord is twisted together with theirs and with the Lord's. Weak strands, bound to Him, become a rope that holds.
