# Daily Devotional — Sunday, 22 November 2026

## Bible Verses About Thanksgiving: Enter Into His Gates With Thanksgiving and Into His Courts With Praise

**Scripture:** *"Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise: be thankful unto him, and bless his name. For the LORD is good; his mercy is everlasting; and his truth endureth to all generations."* — Psalm 100:4–5 (KJV)

**Read:** Psalm 100 · Psalm 103:1–5 · Leviticus 7:11–15 · Acts 16:25–34 · Philippians 4:6–7 · 1 Thessalonians 5:16–18

---

When people look for Bible verses about thanksgiving, Psalm 100 is almost always near the top of the list. It is a short psalm written for worshippers walking up to the house of God, and it tells them how to arrive: "Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise." Thanksgiving is not the last thing we do after God has answered. It is the gate we walk through to meet Him at all.

The Hebrew word for thanksgiving here is todah. It comes from a root that means to stretch out or throw the hand, and it was also the name of a sacrifice: the thank offering a worshipper brought to the altar when God had rescued him (Leviticus 7:12). So todah is thanks you can see. It is a hand lifted, a gift laid down, a confession spoken aloud: "The LORD did this." In Scripture, thanksgiving is never only a private feeling. It is an offering.

The psalm also tells us why we give it. "For the LORD is good; his mercy is everlasting; and his truth endureth to all generations." We do not thank God because every circumstance is good. We thank Him because He is good, and His mercy does not run out when our season changes. Paul and Silas knew this in the Philippian jail. Their backs were bleeding and their feet were in stocks, yet at midnight they prayed and sang praises to God, and the prisoners heard them (Acts 16:25). Thanksgiving in the dark is the loudest sermon a believer can preach.

In our house gatherings we keep a simple habit. Before anyone shares a need, each person names one thing to thank God for from the past week. We also keep a small notebook of prayer requests and the answers that come. After a year that notebook is a record of God's faithfulness, and when a hard week arrives, someone opens it and reads aloud. Faces change. Gratitude remembers what worry forgets.

How do you live this today? Begin your prayer the way the psalm begins worship. Before you ask for anything, thank Him for something. Thank Him first for salvation through Jesus Christ, then for daily bread, for your family, for answered prayers, and even for the prayers He answered differently than you wanted, because He knows best. Then say it aloud. Todah is a hand stretched out, not a thought kept in. Tell your family one thing God did this week, and let praise become the atmosphere of your home.

This is why the Prayer Tower never sleeps. Every hour, somewhere in the world, one believer enters His gates with thanksgiving while another enters with tears, and they hold each other up. Our brothers and sisters in places where faith costs dearly often teach us the most about todah: they thank God for a Bible, for a quiet room, for one more Sunday together. When you take your hour, begin with thanks for them, then thank God for the nations, because His mercy is everlasting and His truth reaches every generation and every land.

**Prayer**

Father, before I ask for anything, I thank You. Thank You for saving my soul through Jesus Christ, for a new heart and a living hope. Thank You for life and breath, for food on my table, for my family, and for every answered prayer, even the ones You answered differently than I wanted. Teach me to enter Your gates with thanksgiving and Your courts with praise, in bright days and at midnight. You are good; Your mercy is everlasting. I will bless Your name today and every day. In Jesus' name, Amen.
