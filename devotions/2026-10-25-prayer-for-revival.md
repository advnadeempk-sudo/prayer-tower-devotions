# Prayer for Revival: Lord, Wilt Thou Not Revive Us Again?

**Daily Devotional — Sunday, 25 October 2026**

**Scripture:** *"Wilt thou not revive us again: that thy people may rejoice in thee?"* — Psalm 85:6 (KJV)

**Read:** Psalm 85:1–7 · Habakkuk 3:1–2 · Haggai 2:1–9 · Acts 4:23–31

---

A prayer for revival is one of the oldest prayers in the Bible, and it was first prayed by people who had already been rescued once. Psalm 85 belongs to a congregation that had come home from captivity. The altar was rebuilt, the songs had started again, yet their hearts were cold and their joy was gone. So they asked the question that still rises from every tired church: "Wilt thou not revive us again: that thy people may rejoice in thee?" Revival is not first for the lost. It is for God's own people who are alive on the outside and dry on the inside.

The Hebrew word translated "revive" is chayah, the ordinary word for "to live". In this form it means "to make alive again", to put breath back into something that is fainting. It is the word used when Jacob's spirit "revived" at the news that Joseph was alive (Genesis 45:27). The psalmist is not asking for a new building or a better programme. He is asking God to breathe life back into His people. And the small word "again" tells us he had seen God do it before. Revival is simply God returning to do what He has always done.

When the exiles first came home they laid the foundation of the temple with shouting. Then opposition came, discouragement set in, and the work stood still for about sixteen years (Ezra 4:24). Nothing was wrong with the stones; something had died in the hearts of the builders. God did not send a new budget. He sent a word: "Be strong, all ye people of the land, saith the LORD, and work: for I am with you" (Haggai 2:4). Every great decline begins in the heart before it shows in the field, and every great restoration begins in the heart as well.

How do we pray for revival today? Start where the psalm starts, with yourself: "Turn us, O God" (Psalm 85:4). Confess the coldness honestly, because God revives the humble, not the impressive. Ask for joy, not only for results, for the psalm's goal is "that thy people may rejoice in thee". Pray the Word back to God; Habakkuk did exactly that when he cried, "O LORD, revive thy work in the midst of the years" (Habakkuk 3:2). And do not wait for a better season. Habakkuk asked for revival in the middle of hard years, not after them.

This is the heartbeat of the 24-hour prayer tower. Habakkuk said, "I will stand upon my watch" (Habakkuk 2:1), and the tower is that watch stretched around the clock and around the world. History shows that revival often visits the suffering church first: when the early believers were threatened, they prayed, the place was shaken, and they spoke the Word with boldness (Acts 4:31). In the villages of Pakistan we see it still, as people who have never held a Bible hear the gospel and call on the name of the Lord. Each hour someone keeps in the tower is a small "Wilt thou not revive us again?" lifted for the nations.

## Prayer

Father, I come as one of Your own people asking the old question: wilt Thou not revive us again? Turn me first. Where my heart has grown cold, breathe Your life into it. Where my hands have grown slack in Your work, say to me as You said to the builders, "I am with you." Give my church joy in Thee, not only activity for Thee. Revive Your work in the midst of these years, in my home, in my nation and in every hidden church that suffers for Your name. Fill the watchmen in the prayer tower this hour with Your Spirit and Your Word. In Jesus' name, Amen.

- Persecuted believers in hostile places, that pressure would become the furnace of revival rather than the grave of their faith, as it was in Acts 4.
- The nations, Pakistan among them, that God would revive His work in the midst of the years.
