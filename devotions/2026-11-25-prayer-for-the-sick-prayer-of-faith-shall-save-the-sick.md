# Prayer for the Sick: The Prayer of Faith Shall Save the Sick

**Scripture:** Is any sick among you? let him call for the elders of the church; and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord: And the prayer of faith shall save the sick, and the Lord shall raise him up; and if he have committed sins, they shall be forgiven him. (James 5:14-15, KJV)

Every pastor knows the late-night message: "Please pray, my mother is in hospital." A prayer for the sick is one of the oldest prayers of the church, and James tells us how to pray it. "Is any sick among you? let him call for the elders of the church." The sick person is not left alone. He calls, the church comes, prayer is offered "in the name of the Lord". In the Bible, healing is a family matter.

Look at the promise: "the prayer of faith shall save the sick." The Greek word translated "save" is sozo. It means to rescue, to make whole. It is the word Jesus used for the woman who had spent all she had on physicians, when she touched His garment: "thy faith hath made thee whole" (Matthew 9:22). It is also the word for the salvation of the soul. The Lord who saves the soul is the same Lord who raises up the body, and verse 15 puts sickness and sin in one breath: "if he have committed sins, they shall be forgiven him."

When I preach on sickness and healing I point to four pictures in the Gospels: the paralysed man let down through the roof, whose sins Jesus forgave before He said "Arise" (Mark 2); the woman whose doctors could not help her (Mark 5); the woman bound eighteen years, loosed in a moment (Luke 13); the man born blind, "that the works of God should be made manifest in him" (John 9). Sickness comes for different reasons, but in every picture the answer is Jesus. Whatever the reason, bring the sick to Him.

In our village churches I have seen a family gather round a seriously ill brother, pray and fast together, and watch God raise him up. I have seen a mother, sleepless for weeks because no doctor could settle her crying baby, finally rest after the pastors prayed in the name of Jesus. Not every prayer is answered as we ask; "the Lord shall raise him up" is fulfilled sometimes here, sometimes in His presence. But the command does not change: pray. The prayer of faith is not faith in our faith; it is faith in the Lord who hears.

So how do you pray for the sick today? Name the person before God and ask plainly for healing in the name of Jesus. Confess your own sins first, for verse 16 says, "pray one for another, that ye may be healed." Then stay close: visit, call, sit by the bed. Prayer and medicine are not enemies; the oil in James was also an everyday remedy, and God uses doctors as well as elders.

In a 24-hour prayer tower, sickness never has to wait for morning. Somewhere a mother sits up with a feverish child at three o'clock. Somewhere a believer in a hostile place is ill and cannot "call for the elders", because no church is within reach. When you take your hour, you become the elders of the church for someone who has no one to call, and the prayer of faith carries them to the Lord.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, You are the same yesterday, today and for ever, and You still heal the sick. I bring before You every person I know who is ill today, and I name them now. Let the prayer of faith save the sick, and raise them up by Your mighty hand. Forgive my sins and theirs, and let Your peace rest on every hospital bed and every sickroom. Give wisdom to the doctors and strength to those who care for the sick. Remember my brothers and sisters who suffer for Your name and have no one to pray over them. I trust You, whatever the answer, for You are the LORD that healeth. In Your holy name I pray. Amen.
