# How to Pray the Lord's Prayer: The Pattern Jesus Gave Us for Talking to the Father

**Date:** Monday, 9 November 2026 · **Theme:** Prayer & intercession

**Scripture:** *"After this manner therefore pray ye: Our Father which art in heaven, Hallowed be thy name. Thy kingdom come. Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread. And forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors. And lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil: For thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory, for ever. Amen."* — Matthew 6:9-13 (KJV)

How to pray the Lord's Prayer is a question older than the church itself. One disciple, having watched Jesus pray, finally asked: "Lord, teach us to pray." His answer was the Lord's Prayer. Notice how He introduced it: "After this manner therefore pray ye." He was not only giving us words to recite. He was giving us a pattern for every conversation with God.

Look at that pattern. The prayer opens with God, not with us. His name, His kingdom and His will come first; only then our needs: bread, forgiveness, protection. Jesus teaches us to lift our eyes before we open our hands. And the first word is "Father". We do not approach a distant judge but a Father who already knows our needs and loves to hear us ask. So prayer must never be kept for days of trouble. Talk to Him as a child talks to a father: thank Him, worship Him, tell Him your fears and your dreams.

In the middle of the prayer sits a word that puzzled scholars for centuries. "Give us this day our daily bread." The Greek word for daily is epiousios. It appears only here and in Luke's version of the prayer, and nowhere else in the surviving Greek writing of that age; an early church teacher believed the Gospel writers coined it. Its sense is bread "for the coming day", enough for today's living. It is manna language: one day's supply, gathered fresh each morning. Jesus is teaching us to depend on the Father one day at a time, and to be content when today's portion is enough.

Now notice the small words. It is never "my bread" or "forgive me"; it is always "our" and "us". You cannot pray the Lord's Prayer alone; intercession is woven into every line. In one village house church a brother told us that learning how to pray was the greatest testimony of his life. Another, a farmer five years in debt, kept asking his fellowship to pray with him, and God cleared it in a single harvest. That is what "give us" sounds like when a church prays it together.

So how do you pray the Lord's Prayer today? Slowly. Take one line at a time and let it open into your own words. "Hallowed be thy name": worship Him for who He is. "Thy kingdom come": name a nation, a city, a neighbour. "Give us this day our daily bread": bring today's needs, yours and someone else's. "Forgive us": name the person you must forgive. "Deliver us from evil": cover your home and your church. Five unhurried minutes like this will change the climate of your day. Win the private battle first, and the public ones will look different.

This is why the 24-hour prayer tower exists. In every hour, in some language, someone is saying "Our Father". For persecuted believers, "Give us this day our daily bread" is prayed by families who lost their income the day they confessed Christ. "Deliver us from evil" is whispered behind locked doors. When you take your hour and pray this prayer, you say "us" with them, and the Father hears one voice.

## Prayer

Our Father in heaven, thank You that I may call You Father. Hallowed be Your name in my heart and in my home today. Let Your kingdom come and Your will be done in my life, in my city and in my nation, as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread; I bring You my own needs and the needs of my brothers and sisters, and I trust You for enough for today. Forgive me my debts, and give me grace to forgive those who have wronged me. Lead me away from temptation and deliver me from every evil. Teach me to pray as Jesus taught, every day and not only in trouble. For Yours is the kingdom, the power and the glory, for ever. In Jesus' name, Amen.
