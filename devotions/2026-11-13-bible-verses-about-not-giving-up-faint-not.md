# Bible Verses About Not Giving Up: In Due Season We Shall Reap, If We Faint Not

**Daily Devotional — Friday, 13 November 2026**

**Scripture:** *"And let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not."* — Galatians 6:9 (KJV)

**Read:** Galatians 6:7–10 · 1 Samuel 30:1–8 · Nehemiah 6:9–16 · Luke 18:1–8 · Hebrews 12:1–3

---

People search for Bible verses about not giving up at the end of a long day, not at the beginning. The job has not come, the sickness has not lifted, the prayer has not been answered, and a quiet voice asks, "Why keep going?" Paul wrote to tired believers: "And let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not" (Galatians 6:9). Notice that he does not deny the weariness. He names it, and then he gives us a reason to stay.

The word translated "faint" is the Greek ekluo. It pictures a bow whose string has gone slack. The bow is not broken, the archer is still standing and the arrows are still in the quiver, but a slack string cannot send anything anywhere. That is what losing heart does. The problem stays exactly the same size; it is the hand that was carrying it that opens and lets go. Discouragement is the enemy's cheapest weapon. He does not need to defeat you. He only needs you to walk off the field.

Think of a farmer who has ploughed, sown and watered through a whole season, and then, one week before the harvest, abandons the field because he cannot yet see the grain. Everything he did was good; he simply stopped too early. Paul's promise carries a timetable: "in due season." Between the seed and the harvest there is always a quiet stretch where nothing seems to be happening. Nothing is being wasted in that stretch. Your prayers, your tears and your faithfulness are all counted, and the season God has fixed is coming.

So how do we tighten the string again? David, standing in the ashes of Ziklag with his own men ready to stone him, "encouraged himself in the LORD his God" (1 Samuel 30:6). He did not wait for someone else to lift him; he reminded himself who God is. Nehemiah, mocked while he built, prayed one short sentence: "O God, strengthen my hands" (Nehemiah 6:9). Pray that sentence today over the thing you are tempted to abandon: a marriage, a wayward child, a ministry, a job search, a sick body. Then take one more step of well doing. Not ten steps; one. God counts the one.

Jesus put prayer and perseverance in the same sentence: men ought always to pray, and not to faint (Luke 18:1). In His mind there are only two roads, keep praying or lose heart. This is why the 24-hour prayer tower never closes. Somewhere tonight a believer is in that silent stretch between the seed and the harvest, and in hostile places our brothers and sisters are being tempted to believe that quiet surrender is the only way left. When you take your hour, you become the voice that says to them across the miles, "Faint not; the due season is near."

## Prayer

Father, You are near to the broken-hearted and You give power to the faint. I bring You my tired heart and my slack hands. Where I have stopped praying, bring me back to my knees; where I have stopped sowing, put the seed back in my hand. Lord Jesus, You endured the cross and did not lose heart; fix my eyes on You. Holy Spirit, fill me not with fear but with power, love and a sound mind. Strengthen my hands, O God, and let me reap in due season. In Jesus' name, Amen.

## Pray with us today

- For every believer who is tired of waiting and tempted to give up: that God would tighten the slack string, renew their strength and put the seed back in their hands.
- For persecuted believers in hostile lands: that they would not faint under pressure, that the Lord would strengthen their hands, and that surrender would never seem the only way left.
- For the nations and for Pakistan: that weary pastors, parents and young people would keep sowing good seed and reap in due season.
