## The Power of Persistent Prayer: Why God Honours Those Who Keep Knocking

**Scripture:** *"And he spake a parable unto them to this end, that men ought always to pray, and not to faint."* — Luke 18:1 (KJV)

**Read:** Luke 18:1–8 · Daniel 10:12–13 · James 5:16

---

Jesus told the story of a widow with no money, no lawyer and no influence. Her only weapon was persistence. Day after day she stood before an unjust judge and said the same words: *"Avenge me of mine adversary."* The judge cared nothing for God or man, yet her refusal to go away wore him down. Then Jesus made the point that still shakes complacent hearts today: if an unjust judge answers persistence, how much more will a loving Father answer His own children who cry to Him day and night?

The Greek word Jesus used for "faint" is *ekkakeō* — to lose heart, to grow weary and give up. Persistent prayer is not nagging God; it is refusing to let discouragement close the door that faith has opened. Daniel prayed for twenty-one days before the answer broke through, and the angel told him that his words were heard on the very first day (Daniel 10:12). Delay is not denial. Heaven was moving while Daniel was still on his knees.

This is why a 24-hour prayer tower exists. Around the world, every hour of the day and night, someone is awake and praying. When you grow tired in Lahore, a believer in London takes up the cry. When London sleeps, Los Angeles is interceding. Together, the body of Christ becomes a single widow who never stops knocking — for persecuted Christians, for broken families, for the sick, for nations in turmoil. *"The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much"* (James 5:16). Fervent prayer is persistent prayer.

Whatever you have been praying for — a prodigal child, a healing, a job, a breakthrough for your church — do not faint today. Jesus ended the parable with a searching question: *"When the Son of man cometh, shall he find faith on the earth?"* Faith is not proved by one loud prayer but by the quiet prayer still being prayed on the thirtieth day.
