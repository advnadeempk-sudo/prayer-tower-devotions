# Intercessory Prayer: Will You Stand in the Gap?

**Daily Devotional — Monday, 26 October 2026**

**Scripture:** *"And I sought for a man among them, that should make up the hedge, and stand in the gap before me for the land, that I should not destroy it: but I found none."* — Ezekiel 22:30 (KJV)

**Read:** Ezekiel 22:23–31 · Exodus 32:7–14, 30–32 · Psalm 106:19–23 · Isaiah 59:16 · 1 Timothy 2:1–4

---

Intercessory prayer is the ministry of standing before God on behalf of someone else. In Ezekiel 22 the city had become like a wall with a hole torn in it; sin had broken the defences and judgment was pouring through. God was not looking for a bigger army or a wiser council. He said, "I sought for a man among them, that should make up the hedge, and stand in the gap before me for the land." Then come four of the saddest words in the Bible: "but I found none." Heaven searched for an intercessor, and the post was empty.

The Hebrew word for intercession is paga. It is an ordinary word meaning to meet, to reach or to fall upon; it describes a traveller arriving at a place, as when Jacob "lighted upon" Bethel (Genesis 28:11). Used of prayer, it means to meet someone with a plea on another person's behalf. Isaiah uses it twice: the Servant "made intercession for the transgressors" (Isaiah 53:12), and God "wondered that there was no intercessor" (Isaiah 59:16). An intercessor, then, is simply a person who goes to meet God carrying a name that is not his own.

Moses shows us what this looks like. When Israel made the golden calf and God spoke of destroying the nation, Moses did not step back from the breach; he stepped into it. "Yet now, if thou wilt forgive their sin; and if not, blot me, I pray thee, out of thy book" (Exodus 32:32). Psalm 106:23 remembers that day: judgment was turned away because "Moses his chosen stood before him in the breach." Anyone who has lived in a village knows what a broken boundary wall means: until it is mended, someone must stand in the gap through the night, or the animals wander and the thieves walk in. Intercessory prayer is standing in that gap for people who cannot stand for themselves.

How do we practise it today? Name the person, because God sought "a man", one person for one need, not a vague wish for the world. Carry the need as your own, as Moses did when he put his name on the line. Pray the Word over the person, turning promises into petitions. Keep standing; Daniel prayed twenty-one days before the answer broke through (Daniel 10:12-13). And begin with those who have no one to pray for them: the sick neighbour, the backslidden friend, the leaders of your country (1 Timothy 2:1-2), and the villages where no one has yet heard the gospel.

This is why the 24-hour prayer tower exists. A wall is only as strong as its weakest hour, and the tower makes sure that at every hour of the day and night someone is standing in the gap. Many of our brothers and sisters live where a public prayer meeting is impossible; we stand for them, remembering "them that are in bonds, as bound with them" (Hebrews 13:3). When you take your hour, you are the answer to God's search in Ezekiel 22. He is still looking, and this time He need not say "I found none."

## Prayer

Lord, You are still seeking someone to stand in the gap, and today I answer: here am I. Teach me intercessory prayer. Give me the heart of Moses, who would not step back from the breach, and the patience of Daniel, who kept praying until the answer came. I bring before You the one whose name is on my heart right now; meet them where I cannot reach. Make up the hedge around every persecuted believer who cannot pray openly today. Turn the hearts of leaders and open the villages that have not yet heard. Let it never be said of this hour that You found none. In Jesus' name, Amen.
